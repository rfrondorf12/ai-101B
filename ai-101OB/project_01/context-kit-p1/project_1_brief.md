# Project 1: Iterate, Then Compare

**Weight:** 15%  
**Final submission:** due Sunday, September 27, 2026 at 11:59 p.m.

## Purpose

Build context for the model inside your vault. Steer the model through iteration toward a finished artifact you can defend. Then compare work across the class: how did differences in context, instructions, and direction produce different outputs?

## Shared Conditions

The instructor supplies one brief, one starting prompt. You may revise the prompt and edit the output. The assignment judges your context, your instructions, and your direction, not how impressive the generated material looks.

## Shared Prompt

STARTING PROMPT
A square poster for a student AI-art pin-up, bold flat shapes, exactly three colors, no text.

## The Work, In Order

1. **Build a context kit.** Before you generate anything, collect material the model can read: reference photos, a color palette, style notes, a short note on what you want the output to feel like. Keep it in a folder in your vault so you can point your model and tool of choice at it. Aim for enough context that the model starts from your taste, not its defaults.
2. **Run a first output.** Use the shared starting prompt. Log what the model returned and what is wrong with it relative to your intent.
3. **Steer for at least three rounds.** Each round, change something on purpose: add context, rewrite the instruction, or give the model a direction (keep this, drop that, push further here). Log every round: what you changed, what came back, and your keep, change, or reject decision with one reason.
4. **Finish the artifact.** One finished response to the shared brief. Hand edits count. The final keep-or-reject call is yours.

## Deliverables

1. **Artifact:** One finished response to the shared brief.
2. **Context kit:** The folder of notes, references, and images the model could see. Name each item and say what job it did.
3. **AI Use Log:** Dated entries for every round. Each entry records the context the model could see, the instruction you gave, the direction you set, the output, and your decision with one reason.
4. **Decision note:** About one page. Name one output you kept, one you rejected, and the reasons for both. Describe what changed between your first output and your final artifact, and which steering move caused the change. Add one observation from the class lineup that traces a visible difference to a difference in context, instructions, or direction.
5. **Disclosure:** One sentence that identifies the AI contribution and your contribution.
6. **Workspace:** An organized folder pushed to a GitHub repository shared with the instructor.

New to Markdown or Obsidian? Follow the Markdown, Obsidian, and Claudian guide to create the required files and check any AI-assisted changes.
